# Supplementary methods

## Study material and count matrix

This reanalysis uses bulk RNA sequencing data generated by de Castro et al. for patients with myeloproliferative neoplasms (MPN) sampled before and after transformation to secondary acute myeloid leukaemia (sAML). The original study describes flow sorted peripheral blood cells depleted of T and B lymphocytes. The undergraduate research report documents paired end 75 bp sequencing on an Illumina NextSeq 500, read quality assessment with FastQC and MultiQC, trimming with Trimmomatic, alignment to GRCh38 with STAR, and gene counting with HTSeq. The present repository begins with the supplied merged **integer gene count matrix**; it does not rerun processing from FASTQ files. Access to the original processed dataset is managed through the restricted Zenodo record [10.5281/zenodo.1212754](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.1212754).

Sample metadata identify 12 patients with paired chronic MPN and post transformation samples (24 progressor samples) and five patients with two samples who did not progress (10 non progressor samples). The analysis includes these 34 metadata matched samples. `Amostra_18` remains in the input count matrix and sample quality control output but is excluded from downstream analyses because it has no matching metadata. The non progressor samples are labelled `NP first` and `NP second` and are used only for descriptive displays and CIBERSORTx summaries; they do not enter the paired differential expression contrast or gene ranking.

## Differential expression and expression plots

Rows beginning with `__` (HTSeq technical categories) are removed and Ensembl version suffixes are stripped from gene identifiers. An archived Ensembl annotation table supplies gene symbols and chromosome assignments. Genes without symbols remain eligible for differential expression, whereas annotated X and Y chromosome genes are excluded. Filtering is based **only on the 24 progressor samples**: a gene must have at least 10 counts in at least six samples. Filtered counts for the progressor pairs are analysed using DESeq2 with design `~ Patient_ID + Condition`. `Condition` has `pre` as its reference level; the contrast is `pos` versus `pre`, so a positive log2 fold change indicates higher expression after transformation. `results()` uses `alpha = 0.10` for independent filtering. The reported DEG set requires an adjusted P value below 0.05 and an absolute log2 fold change above 0.5; the adjusted P criterion alone is reported separately. The older total count >30 / five nonzero sample filter described in the undergraduate report is **not** used in this reanalysis.

For plots that include both progressor and non progressor samples, size factors and a variance stabilising transformation (VST; `blind = FALSE`) are computed across the 34 filtered samples with a sampling point design. This visualisation transform does not alter the paired DESeq2 fit. Figure 1B starts from a predefined 79 gene biological module list, retains module genes that meet the DEG criteria, and displays each gene's VST expression as a row Z score across the 34 samples, without clustering or reordering the predefined module rows. PCA and t-SNE in Supplementary Figure S1 use only the 24 progressor samples: PCA uses the 500 most variable VST genes; t-SNE uses all filtered VST genes, perplexity 5, seed 2026, and the other settings recorded in `analysis.Rmd`.

## Preranked gene set enrichment analysis

Each gene with a finite unadjusted DESeq2 P value and finite log2 fold change is eligible for ranking, whether or not it passes a DEG threshold. Gene identifiers remain Ensembl IDs; genes are not converted to or deduplicated by gene symbol for GSEA. The two comparator ranks are `sign(L) × −log10(p*)` (confidence) and `L × −log10(p*)` (impact), where `L` is the post versus pre log2 fold change and `p* = max(p, .Machine$double.xmin)` prevents infinite scores when an unadjusted P value is zero.

The hybrid rank used for the main GSEA results is exactly:

$$
r_{\mathrm{hybrid}}=\operatorname{sign}(L)
\begin{cases}
-\log_{10}(0.05)+|L|, & p^*<0.05,\\
-\log_{10}(p^*)+|L|, & 0.05\leq p^*<0.10,\\
-\log_{10}(p^*), & p^*\geq0.10.
\end{cases}
$$

Thus effect magnitude contributes below P = 0.10; for P ≥ 0.10 only direction and statistical evidence contribute. The first two branches meet at P = 0.05. **The score has a discontinuity at P = 0.10** because the effect magnitude is dropped at that boundary. This is the implemented rule, not a continuous interpolation. Rankings are sorted in decreasing order and passed to `fgsea::fgseaMultilevel` with `scoreType = "std"`, `eps = 1e-10`, `nproc = 1`, and seed 2026. The frozen Ensembl ID gene sets in `inputs/reference/msigdb_7.5.1_ensembl.rds` come from MSigDB version 7.5.1 Hallmark and KEGG collections. Set size limits are 10–500 for Hallmark and 15–500 for KEGG. Pathways with BH adjusted P < 0.05 are displayed; a positive normalised enrichment score indicates enrichment after progression. The three rankings are compared using intersections of their significant pathways within each collection (Supplementary Figure S4); pathway count alone is not a measure of ranking accuracy. Files from the separate 2025.1 gene set download in `inputs/reference/` are **not** used by this GSEA run.

## CIBERSORTx estimates and comparisons

The deconvolution **was performed before this R Markdown workflow**. The undergraduate report identifies `AML_GSE116256_Expression`, derived from the Van Galen et al. AML single cell reference, as the signature matrix. `analysis.Rmd` reads the supplied CIBERSORTx results CSV; it does not submit a new job or recover unrecorded options from the result table. The six reported states are Monocyte, NK, GMP, Progenitor, Malignant and Promonocyte. Their fractions are checked to be between zero and one and to sum to one per sample. The supplied CIBERSORTx P value, correlation and RMSE are retained in Table S6, **without filtering samples by these fields**. GMP is retained even though its estimated fraction is zero in all analysed samples.

Figures 2A and 2B display, respectively, individual sample fractions and group means with individual values for pre and post progressor samples and the first and second non progressor samples. These four group comparisons are descriptive. For Supplementary Figure S5 and Table S8, within patient pre versus post comparisons are carried out for the 12 progressors using paired Wilcoxon signed rank tests (`exact = FALSE`, continuity correction enabled), followed by Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment across testable cell states. A state with all paired differences equal to zero has no test P value. The earlier report also discusses LinDeconSeq; that analysis is **not** part of the present figures or pipeline.

## Software and reproducibility

`analysis.Rmd` and `config.yml` are the executable specifications for the reanalysis. DESeq2 performs differential expression and VST; fgsea performs enrichment; Rtsne performs t-SNE; pheatmap, ggplot2, cowplot and patchwork assemble the figures. The workflow saves gene rankings, full DESeq2 and GSEA tables, descriptive CIBERSORTx tables, analysis checkpoints, input checksums, `sessionInfo()` and installed package versions under `outputs/` and `checkpoints/` when rendered. The generated directories may be excluded from Git; reproducing the work requires access to authorised input data. Package citations can be obtained in R with `citation("DESeq2")`, `citation("fgsea")`, `citation("Rtsne")` and similarly for other packages. The report documents upstream FASTQ processing, whereas the executable repository starts with merged counts and the precomputed CIBERSORTx table.
