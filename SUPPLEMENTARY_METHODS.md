# Supplementary methods and output guide

This document describes the analysis implemented in [`scripts/analysis.R`](scripts/analysis.R) and the figures and tables generated by the workflow. The analysis uses a merged count matrix, sample metadata, and precomputed CIBERSORTx estimates.

## Samples and count preparation

The RNA-sequencing data were generated by de Castro et al. from flow-sorted peripheral-blood cells depleted of T and B lymphocytes. The original data are available under [restricted access at Zenodo](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.1212754). The upstream protocol used paired-end 75-bp NextSeq 500 sequencing, FastQC/MultiQC, Trimmomatic, STAR alignment to GRCh38, and HTSeq gene counting.

The merged count matrix contains 34 samples: 24 paired samples from 12 patients who progressed from chronic-phase MPN to sAML and 10 samples from five non-progressor patients, each sampled twice. Non-progressor samples are included in expression heatmaps and cell-state summaries as descriptive comparators.

HTSeq technical rows beginning with `__` are removed, and Ensembl version suffixes are stripped from gene IDs. Gene symbols and chromosome annotations are retrieved from the archived Ensembl reference table. The expression filter is applied to the 24 progressor samples, retaining genes with at least 10 counts in at least six samples. Genes annotated on chromosomes X/Y are excluded. Table S11 documents filtering and annotation, and Table S1 reports gene-assigned counts for each sample.

## Paired differential expression and expression plots

Differential expression is assessed using DESeq2 with a paired design (`~ Patient_ID + Condition`) and a post-transformation (`pos`) versus pre-transformation (`pre`) contrast. Positive log2 fold changes indicate higher expression after sAML transformation. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) are defined by Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted *P* < 0.05 and |log2 fold change| > 0.5. Tables S2 and S3 contain the full DESeq2 results and the selected DEGs, respectively. In the Figure 1A volcano plot, genes with adjusted *P* < 0.05 are coloured blue when log2FC < −0.5, red when log2FC > 0.5, and purple when |log2FC| ≤ 0.5; all remaining genes are grey.

For heatmaps, the 34 samples with metadata undergo size-factor normalisation and variance-stabilising transformation (VST; `blind = FALSE`) using sampling point as the visualisation design. Expression is standardised to a within-gene Z score. The 79-gene candidate panel for Figure 1B was manually curated from genes discussed in the study’s biological interpretation of MPN-to-sAML progression and assigned to seven functional modules: myeloid identity loss; myeloid sensors and effectors; cytokines and chemokines; adhesion, migration and antigen presentation; malignant proliferation and DNA repair; paradoxical oncogenes; and survival and RNA metabolism. The complete panel and module assignments are provided in [`inputs/gene_modules.csv`](inputs/gene_modules.csv). Figure 1B includes panel genes meeting the DEG thresholds, retains module order and groups samples by sampling point. Table S9 records module membership and inclusion in the figure. Figure S2 displays thresholded DEGs with mapped symbols, retaining the Ensembl entry with the lowest adjusted *P* when symbols are duplicated; genes are hierarchically clustered and columns remain in sampling-point order.

Figure S1 assesses the 24 progressor samples using VST expression. PCA uses the 500 most variable genes and `prcomp()` with centred, unscaled sample vectors. t-SNE uses all retained genes with `Rtsne`, perplexity 5, PCA initialisation, seed 2026, `theta = 0.5`, 1,000 iterations, and one thread. Figure S3 displays gene-assigned counts for the 35 original samples.

## Preranked gene set enrichment analysis

GSEA is performed using all genes with finite unadjusted DESeq2 *P* values and log2 fold changes, ranked by Ensembl ID. Let `L` denote the post-versus-pre log2 fold change and `p` the unadjusted *P* value, with `q = max(p, .Machine$double.xmin)` for numerical stability. The primary analysis uses the following hybrid score:

```math
r_{\mathrm{hybrid}}=\mathrm{sign}(L)
\begin{cases}
-\log_{10}(0.05)+|L|, & q<0.05,\\
-\log_{10}(q)+|L|, & 0.05\leq q<0.10,\\
-\log_{10}(q), & q\geq0.10.
\end{cases}
```

The hybrid ranking was developed to balance statistical evidence and the magnitude of differential expression. Confidence-based ranking can prioritise small expression changes with highly significant *P* values, whereas impact-based ranking can be strongly influenced by large but uncertain fold-change estimates. The hybrid metric therefore assigns different roles to these quantities according to statistical evidence. For `p ≥ 0.10`, ranking depends on statistical evidence alone because the estimated fold-change magnitude is less well supported. Between `0.05 ≤ p < 0.10`, both statistical evidence and magnitude contribute additively. For `p < 0.05`, the statistical component is fixed at `−log10(0.05)`, and the absolute log2 fold change determines the ranking within each direction of regulation. The score is continuous at `p = 0.05` and discontinuous at `p = 0.10`.

Two alternative rankings were assessed for comparison:

```math
\begin{aligned}
r_{\mathrm{confidence}} &= \mathrm{sign}(L)[-\log_{10}(q)],\\
r_{\mathrm{impact}} &= L[-\log_{10}(q)].
\end{aligned}
```

The original exploratory comparison of these approaches showed that the hybrid ranking recovered additional significant cancer-associated pathways, including mitotic spindle, Notch signalling, and unfolded protein response programmes. Figure S4 and Table S10 show the overlaps and differences among pathways identified by the three metrics. These comparisons informed the selection of the hybrid score for the main analysis.

GSEA uses frozen MSigDB 7.5.1 Hallmark and KEGG gene sets with Ensembl identifiers from `inputs/reference/msigdb_7.5.1_ensembl.rds`. The analysis runs `fgsea::fgseaMultilevel` with `scoreType = "std"`, `eps = 1e-10`, `nproc = 1`, and seed 2026. Gene-set size ranges are 10–500 for Hallmark and 15–500 for KEGG. Pathways with BH-adjusted *P* < 0.05 are considered significant. Positive normalised enrichment scores (NES) indicate enrichment after transformation; negative NES indicate enrichment before transformation. Figures 1C and 1D display the significant KEGG and Hallmark pathways. Tables S4 and S5 provide all tested pathways.

## Cell-state estimates and paired comparisons

CIBERSORTx estimates were imported from `inputs/cibersortx_job40.csv`. The analysis used the `AML_GSE116256_Expression` signature derived from the AML single-cell reference of Van Galen et al. The six estimated cell states are Monocyte, NK, GMP, Progenitor, Malignant, and Promonocyte. Their estimated fractions are checked to fall between zero and one and sum to one within each sample. GMP estimates were zero throughout the supplied dataset.

Figure 2A shows the estimated cell-state composition of the 34 samples across four sampling-point groups; Figure 2B summarises group means and individual estimates. Tables S6 and S7 contain sample-level fractions and group summaries.

Figure S5 and Table S8 compare the 12 progressor pairs using two-sided paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (`exact = FALSE`, continuity correction enabled), with BH adjustment across testable cell states. GMP has no test *P* value because its paired differences are all zero. No state reached adjusted *P* < 0.05. The estimated changes are interpreted as exploratory trends.

## Reproducibility

The pipeline is contained in [`scripts/analysis.R`](scripts/analysis.R), with parameters defined in its `cfg` list. The software environment is recorded in [`environment.yml`](environment.yml). From the repository root, the analysis can be run with:

```bash
conda env create -f environment.yml
conda activate MPN_sAML
Rscript --vanilla scripts/analysis.R
```

The workflow exports figures, tables, checkpoints, input checksums, package versions, parameters, and `sessionInfo()`. Its input reference files are archived under `inputs/reference/`, with source information in `inputs/reference/sources.json`. The merged count matrix requires authorised access under the original study's data-sharing conditions. Package and software citations are provided in the [README](README.md).
